README

Practice & "To learn"

Every card sits in a box from 0 to 5, a spaced-repetition schedule. New cards start in box 0, due immediately. To learn on the home screen is simply the count of cards whose due date has already passed - box 0 cards plus anything whose review interval has come back around.

Tapping Practice → Start practicing studies only those due cards by default. If nothing is due, you'll see a "Nothing due right now" screen with a Practice all cards anyway option, which is the only way to study cards that aren't due yet.

Answering Knew it moves a card up one box and pushes its due date out (box intervals: 1, 3, 7, 16, 35 days). Answering Didn't know (or typing the wrong word) drops it straight back to box 0, due immediately, and requeues it in the same session: the first miss brings it back after at least 10 other cards (or at the very end if fewer than 10 remain), and missing it again moves it into a review pass at the end of the session under a "Let's review the cards you didn't know" heading, where it keeps recycling until you get it right.

The "done" count and progress bar only advance on a correct answer - a card you missed stays counted as remaining (however many times it recycles) until you actually get it right, so the bar always reflects real progress rather than raw attempts.

Mastery bar

The mastery bar groups every card into one of three tiers based on its box: New (box 0), Learning (box 1-2), and Mastered (box 3+). Tapping a segment or its legend opens Manage cards pre-filtered to that tier.

Practice modes

Choose Flip Foreign word, Flip Translation, Type the Foreign word, or Match the words in Practice setup - swipe the carousel to see them all. Your last choice, along with your deck selection, is remembered for next time.

In Flip Foreign word and Flip Translation, tap the card to flip it, then swipe right (or tap Knew it) or swipe left (or tap Didn't know) to answer.

In Type the Foreign word, the card shows the translation - type the foreign word into the field below it, then tap Check (enabled once you've typed something) to flip the card and check your answer, or tap I don't know to reveal it straight away without guessing. Checking ignores case, accent marks, and ¡¿!? punctuation, so typing without a keyboard for that language still counts as correct. There's no Knew it / Didn't know here: a correct typed answer counts as knew it, an incorrect one or I don't know counts as didn't know, then tap Next to continue.

In Match the words, tap a foreign word then its translation to pair them up - correct pairs fade away, a wrong guess flashes red and you try again. This mode is just a quick warm-up: it never changes a card's due date or mastery, so it can't count as "learnt" (or missed) the way the other modes do.

Once a card is flipped, a pencil icon appears in its top-right corner - tap it to edit that card without leaving the lesson. Saving your changes (or backing out without saving) drops you right back into the same lesson, on the same card, with your progress untouched.

Card decks

Decks are tags you can put on a card - a card can belong to any number of decks, or none. Each deck gets a random color as soon as you create it, shown as a tinted glass background on its row and on its tag chips wherever it's listed, so it's easy to spot at a glance.

Practice setup and Manage cards both let you filter by deck, including a "No card deck" bucket for untagged cards. In Practice setup, use the small Select all / Unselect chips to quickly check or clear every deck - Start practicing stays disabled until at least one is selected. Tapping a deck from the Card decks screen jumps straight into Manage cards filtered to it. Deleting a deck removes that tag from its cards - the cards themselves stay put.

In Add card, Ask Gemini to create a deck (at the top) opens a small form (theme, word count, translation language, deck name and your Gemini API key) - tap Create deck and the app asks Gemini directly, then adds the new deck and its cards (each with pronunciation and an example sentence in the notes) and opens it in Cards. Words you already have aren't added twice; they just join the new deck. The key is free from aistudio.google.com/apikey and lives in Settings → AI (or the form asks for it the first time); it stays on this device. If Coffee Shelf already has a key, it's used until you enter your own. This button only appears when adding a brand-new card, not while editing an existing one.

Manage cards (Cards tab)

Search (the ⓧ in the field clears it) and filter by deck or mastery, then tap any card's row to open a flip-able preview - same size and style as the practice card - to check its translation and notes without leaving the list. Swipe a row left to reveal Edit / Delete for that card. Tap Select to check off several cards and delete them together - Select all toggles to Unselect all once everything's checked - or tap + Add card (below the filters) to add a new one; this is now the only way into Add card, which no longer has its own tab. Editing a card shows a Delete option in its own top-right corner instead.

Card of the day

The home screen features one card, picked at random and kept the same all day, filling the page below Mastery. Tap it to flip between the word and its translation/notes, just like in practice.

Notes

The Notes tab holds any number of freeform notes, shown as a grid of cards. Tap + to add one, tap a note to open and edit it - it saves automatically - and use Delete in its top-right corner to remove it. Notes travel with Export/Import alongside your cards, so they survive moving to a new device.

Google Drive backup

Settings (the gear on Home) → Google Drive backup → Sign in with Google. The app talks to Google directly (no server in between) and keeps one backup file in a hidden app folder in your Google Drive - it can't see any of your other files. While connected, it backs up by itself a few seconds after each change. Google's sign-in lasts about an hour; after that, a "Changes not backed up" reminder on the home screen reconnects with one tap.

On a new phone, signing in finds the existing backup and asks whether to Restore it here or Replace with this phone - nothing is overwritten until you choose. Restoring replaces everything on the phone with the backup. Once Drive backup is set up, the weekly "export your cards" reminder stops.

Export / Import

Export saves a plain-text file in Anki's tab-separated note format (word, translation, notes, deck tags, box, due date, reviewed), so it opens directly in Anki too. Deck memberships are stored as deck::Name tags - a card in several decks gets several tags. Your notes are saved in a header comment line.

Import reads that same format (older JSON exports still work too). Matching is by word + translation, case-insensitive: a duplicate isn't re-added, but any new deck tags on it are merged in. Missing decks mentioned in tags are created automatically. If it's been a week since your last export, the app will offer to back up on launch. Imported notes are only adopted if you have none yet - so importing cards never overwrites progress you're already building on this device.

Google Drive backup

Sign in with Google and the app keeps a backup of your cards, decks, progress and notes in a hidden app folder in your Google Drive. It can't see your other files.

Backs up by itself a few seconds after each change while connected. Google keeps the connection for about an hour; after that one tap reconnects.
